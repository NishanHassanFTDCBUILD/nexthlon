Good animation is the kind you don't notice. You just feel the website is fast and alive.

This week I've been building the motion for Nexthlon, the sportswear store I'm making. I started out wanting everything to move. I ended up learning when motion should stay still.

Here are the five ideas that changed how I design animations:

🎯 Motion should explain something. A product card that grows slightly when you hover tells you "you can click this". A spinning logo tells you nothing.

⏱️ Fast beats fancy. Most of my interface animations now last 200–400 ms. Anything longer starts to feel like waiting.

👀 Start the animation when people can see it. My 3D showroom fly-through used to play while it was still off screen, so nobody saw it. Now it begins only when the section scrolls into view.

⚡ Animate the cheap properties. Moving and fading elements (transform and opacity) stays smooth even on a budget phone. Changing width or height makes the page stutter.

♿ Respect "reduce motion". Some people get dizzy from movement and turn it off in their phone settings. A good website listens to that setting.

The lesson I'm taking away: animation is part of the user experience, not decoration on top of it. Every movement should help someone understand or use the page.
